2026-10-02.**

## The short version

Your library is a folder of ordinary files on your own device. Waffle reads and
writes that folder, and works fully without an account.

If you choose to, you can sign in and turn on **Personal Sync** (your library on
all your devices) and **shared folders** (a folder you share with people you
invite). Both are end-to-end encrypted: the contents of your notes and files,
and their names, are encrypted on your device with keys only your devices hold.
Some information is not encrypted — your email address, when you signed in,
sizes and times, and for shared folders who shares with whom. It is all listed
below, because "encrypted" does not mean "invisible".

There is no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no advertising and no
tracking of any kind in the app.

## Your library lives on your device

- **Your files are the real thing.** Notes are Markdown files; everything else
  is an ordinary file in an ordinary folder. You can open the folder with any
  other program without Waffle running.
- **Waffle's index is disposable.** To be fast, Waffle keeps an index of your
  library in the app's own storage, never inside your library. It can be
  rebuilt from your files at any time.
- **Deleting goes to Trash first.** Deleting moves a file to a Trash folder
  inside your library, where you can restore it. Trash empties itself after 30
  days, and "Delete forever" removes something sooner. Those are the only ways
  Waffle deletes a file for good. "Free up space" removes only this device's
  downloaded copy of a file Sync can give back.
- **Waffle does not measure or meter your local library.**
- **In a web browser, storage is weaker.** The web app keeps your library inside
  the browser, where you cannot see it as files, cannot back it up, and the
  browser may clear it. The web app is meant for trying Waffle and saving
  things quickly; the Mac app keeps your library in a real folder.

## Everywhere the app connects to

The web app tells your browser the complete list of places it may connect to,
and the browser refuses everything else:

| Destination | Why | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| app.waffle.page | Loading the app | Every visit |
| Supabase, Waffle's database provider | Sign-in, and the records of Sync and shared folders | Only once you open your account, are already signed in, or open a sign-in link |
| Cloudflare R2, where the encrypted bytes of your files are stored | Uploading and downloading your files, encrypted | Only with Sync or a shared folder on |
| Hugging Face | Downloading the AI models that run on your device | Only after you agree to a specific model |
| OpenFreeMap | Map tiles | Only while a map is open |

**Pictures are the exception.** The app may show pictures from any web
address. If a note contains a picture hosted elsewhere, opening the note asks
that host for it — on every device the note syncs to. Pages Waffle saves for
you have such pictures turned into plain text for this reason, but a note you
write or paste can still contain one.

**Model downloads carry nothing of yours** — only the public model files, each
checked against a fixed fingerprint before use.

**Map tiles reveal roughly where your items are.** The map says so: tiles load
only while it is open, and the area you view hints at your items' locations.

## Accounts

An account is optional and always will be. Signing in uploads nothing; Sync and
shared folders are separate steps you take on purpose.

- **You sign in with a link we email you.** There is no password, so none is
  stored. The email is sent through Postmark.
- **What we keep about your account:** your email address and an account
  number; for each sign-in, when it started and was last renewed, and the
  device's browser description and IP address; and when your email address
  last changed (the time only, not the old address), because a deletion
  request from the website is refused for 7 days after a change.
- **Your choices are recorded, never guessed.** Turning Sync or sharing on is
  saved on your device as a decision, so nothing can switch it on by itself.

## Personal Sync

Personal Sync keeps your library on all your devices. Your files stay on your
devices; what travels between them, through our servers, is encrypted first.

**What our servers can see:** your account and the numbers that identify your
libraries, devices and their approvals; your devices' public keys (the halves
meant to be shared); the size of each change and when it arrived; which item
each change belongs to, and whether it was a deletion; how much storage you use;
and the encrypted changes themselves.

**What our servers never receive:** the contents or names of your notes and
files, your encryption keys, or your recovery code.

**The limits of encryption.** Encryption keeps your content unreadable to our
storage providers acting alone. It cannot guarantee that a server always shows
your devices every latest change: a misbehaving server could hold back or
repeat changes, though it still could not read them. And the pattern of when,
and how much, you sync is visible.

**Where it is stored.** The database and sign-in are run by Supabase in the
United States. The encrypted bytes of your files are stored in Cloudflare R2,
in R2's United States jurisdiction.

**History.** Sync keeps the encrypted history of every note and file — each
earlier version, and the record that something was deleted — for as long as
your account exists, so that you can go back in time. One exception: the
encrypted bytes of a file version are removed from storage 30 days after the
version is replaced, or 30 days after the file is deleted. For a deleted file
the 30 days start when the deletion reaches our servers, not when the file
leaves your Trash: emptying Trash or "Delete forever" does not remove them
sooner, and restoring a file from Trash does not depend on them, because your
device uploads it again. Not removed this way yet: every file deleted
together with its folder (deleting a folder is common, so this is not rare)
keeps its encrypted bytes, and they count towards your storage, until you
delete your account; so, more rarely, does a file deleted while another device
was changing it.

**Turning Sync off on a device** stops that device syncing. It does not delete
what our servers hold for your account; deleting your account does.

**If you lose everything, it is gone.** A new device is approved by one of your
devices or by your recovery code. If you lose every device and the recovery
code, your encrypted data cannot be recovered — by you or by us.

## Shared folders

A shared folder works differently from your own library: our servers hold the
folder, encrypted for its members, and each member's devices keep their own
copy.

**Shared folders reveal more than Sync does.** Our servers can see:

- that an invitation exists, who sent it, and when it expires;
- who tried to accept which invitation — including invitations that were never
  approved. This cannot be hidden, because recording who accepted is what stops
  an invitation being reused;
- which folder each encrypted item belongs to, who added it and when, and its
  size;
- who is a member of which folder, with what role, since when, and when that
  ended;
- when members' keys change;
- who handed a folder over to whom, and when.

**Other members are other people.** What you add to a shared folder is readable
on every member's devices. Removing someone stops them seeing new changes; it
cannot take back what their devices already hold.

**Storage for a shared folder counts against its owner**, not against every
member.

## AI features

AI in Waffle runs on your device. Using your own key with an outside AI service
is optional and never happens by itself.

- **Search, image recognition and transcription run on your device**, using
  models downloaded only after you agree.
- **Transcripts and other AI results stay on your device.** They are not
  shared or synced.
- **In a web browser, outside AI services cannot be used at all**: the web app
  has nowhere to keep a key.
- **If you use your own key in the Mac or phone app**, each request first shows
  the provider, the model, what will be sent, how long the provider keeps it
  (or that it has not said), and the estimated time and cost. Only what you
  selected is sent. Health, financial, contact and password-type data stays on
  your device by default.
- **No account is needed for any of this.**

## Saving things from the web

**The app itself does not look pages up.** Saving a link saves the link; Waffle
does not fetch the page, its title or its icon, because that would tell someone
what you save.

**The browser extension** works only on the tab you click it on. It reads that
page's address, title and the summary the page publishes about itself — not the
text of the page. It cannot see your other tabs, your history or your cookies.

**Two requests happen after you save, and they are named here.**

- **The extension fetches the page's preview picture**, without cookies and
  without telling that server which page you were on.
- **On iPhone and iPad, the app may fetch one preview picture** a few minutes
  after you share a page to Waffle, if the page named one. That server sees
  your IP address and the time, and nothing that identifies you or Waffle: no
  cookies, no account, no device or language details.

## Payments

Personal Sync is a paid add-on. Prices and plans are not set yet, and none is
stated here until they are.

When you pay, the payment is handled by whoever you pay through: **Stripe** on
the web and in the Mac app, **Apple** in the iPhone and iPad app, and **Google**
if the Android app sells it. Your card or account details go to them, never to
Waffle. They receive your email address, the plan, the amount and what they
need for tax; Waffle learns from them whether your subscription is active and
until when. Each keeps its own payment records for as long as the law requires,
under its own privacy policy.

A subscription bought through Apple or Google is managed and cancelled there;
deleting your Waffle account does not cancel it.

## Deletion

**On your device.** Deleting a note or file moves it to Trash inside your own
library, where you can restore it. Trash empties itself after 30 days, and
"Delete forever" removes something sooner. Removing Waffle does not delete your
library, and you do not need Waffle to read it.

**Your account.** You can delete your account in the app (Account → Delete
account), or at waffle.page/delete-account with only your email address.

- **A 7-day hold comes first.** Deleting your account starts a 7-day hold.
  During it your account is frozen: Sync stops on every device, and signing in
  offers only "Cancel deletion". We email you when the hold starts, with a link
  to cancel, and again the day before deletion. Cancelling restores the account
  as it was.
- **Then everything Waffle stores for the account is deleted, keys first.** The
  keys we hold for your account are destroyed before anything else, so what
  remains cannot be opened by a new device. Then your synced notes and files and
  their history, your devices and sign-ins, every shared folder you own that
  nobody else uses, and the account itself are deleted.
- **What is kept, and for how long** — the same list the deletion page and the
  app's deletion receipt give:
  - **30 days:** our nightly backup still holds your account's records until it expires.
  - **Indefinitely:** a random number that identified your account, and when it was deleted, so no new account can take its place. It contains nothing about you.
  - **For the life of the folder:** in shared folders other people own, the record that you were a member — your invitations, your access (ended, not erased) and what you added there are the other members' to keep.
  - **For the life of those folders:** the public keys other members use to check what you shared with them. They open nothing.
  - **Until its current owner erases it:** a folder you started and handed to someone else still names you as the one who started it. A folder you owned alone is deleted, leaving only a short fingerprint so it cannot be recreated.
  - **Indefinitely:** a shared folder whose hand-over was left unfinished, which nobody can now erase. The receipt names any such folder.
  - **For our database provider's own period:** its sign-in audit log, which records each sign-in with its IP address and the account it belongs to. Waffle cannot delete it or see how long it is kept; during the beta the provider keeps its logs about a day, and from public launch about a week.
  - **For our database provider's own period:** its own backups — none during the beta, and from public launch its daily backups, kept about a week.
- **A shared folder you own that other people still use** has to be handed
  over or erased first, from a device where you are signed in; deletion stops
  and names it until you do.
- **What stays with you.** Your files on your own devices — Waffle never deletes
  them for you. One exception: a file you removed from a device with "Free up
  space" exists only on our servers until a device downloads it again, so
  deleting your account deletes it. Open such files on a device first if you
  want to keep them. A device where you are still signed in keeps its own copy
  of your keys until you remove Waffle's data there, and a recovery code you
  wrote down is still on paper.

## Backups

Every night the database is copied — every record, including account email
addresses and the encrypted Sync and shared-folder records — and the copy is
kept privately at GitHub for 30 days, then deleted. The copy is encrypted with
a key Waffle holds, and leaves out sign-in sessions. Your content inside it is
encrypted either way. A deleted account stays in the backups until they
expire.
